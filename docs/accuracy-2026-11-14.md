# Accuracy — what is measured, and what "today" can mean

**Question:** how accurate is this trading system, as of Saturday 2026-11-14?

**Short answer:** there is no measurement of today's signals, because today the
system made no prediction — the plan reads `NO_TRADE` with 0 candidates on
breadth 0.1827, and it read that from bars ending **2026-11-06**. The accuracy it
owns is a study result: **76.32% win / +1.85% a trade over 76 corrected
out-of-sample trades in 3 of 6 folds**, expectancy **+0.04%** once the study's own
survivorship haircut is applied. Quote that as the study's number, never as
tonight's.

## 1. The corrected headline holds up — in the study's prose, not a CSV

The owning table is `docs/21-screener-spec.md:11–16`: trades 79→**76**, win
78.48%→**76.32%** (Wilson [65.6%, 84.5%]), mean +2.91%→**+1.85%**, pf 1.42→1.24,
maxDD 22.5%→**9.4%**. `docs/21:211` fixes the population: "on 76 trades across
f2/f3/f4". Three folds, not six — folds f0, f1 and f5 never reach breadth 0.60 at
all (`docs/24-trade-count-ceiling.md` §1).

No results CSV carries these: `results/top10_methods.csv` and
`results/method_table.csv` still hold the pre-correction rows, which is why
`backend/app/plan/citations.py` cites them with file, line and regeneration
command. That is defensible; it is also why the number cannot be re-derived from
an artifact in one step.

The interval is the point. 76 trades at 76.32% gives a 19-point Wilson interval,
and the study's own episode bootstrap is wider still:
`results/episode_block_ci.csv` reports all 57 replay tier-1 trades falling inside
**one** breadth episode, with a block CI of **56.0–100%** win and **−11.1 to
+12.5%** per trade. Effective sample size is closer to one regime than to 76
independent bets.

three times in four and earns four basis points is a lead generator, not a book.

## 3. The searched maximum is a maximum

81.07% / +5.51% on **523 trades** is `results/top10_methods.csv` rank 19
(`breadth_quiet4_rs90_donchian`, `lt_14_40_95`, 20 cells, 5 folds, per-fold
81.4→92.06→…→59.62%). It is the widest row that cleared both bars out of a census
of thousands. `results/multiple_testing.csv` prices that: white reality check
p = **0.4008**, Hansen SPA p = **0.0655**, deflated Sharpe **0** for this family.
The app shows it beside the headline and labels it `searched_census`; it must
never be the number a user plans around.

## 4. The replay, and the one number that is misquoted

`results/five_year_replay_flat.csv` (2021-10 → 2026-10, one window, universe fixed
at the final date): tier 1 **57 trades, 80.70% win, +2.48%**, a 10-slot book taking
18 of them to **+47.90%** with a **68.17%** max drawdown; pooled **−80.52%**.

The README says "the five-year replay books **−68.2%** for tier 1 and −80.5%
pooled". The pooled figure is right; **−68.2% is tier 1's max drawdown, not its
book return**. As written the sentence reverses the sign of the only replayed
result that made money. Flag for correction.

## 5. Capitulation: two answers, one doc

`docs/20-illiquid-and-adaptive-screener.md` §8 carries the attractive row —
**131 trades, 79.39%, +7.26%** — and §7, in the same document, records the

## 6. The only "today" number: 49.4%, which is a coin flip

The flow proxy is the one thing that can be scored against live-ish bars.
`backend/app/flow_accuracy.py:77` (`baseline_signal_accuracy`) takes
sign(`inst_flow_ma`) against sign of the 5-session forward return, per ticker, and
reports N, hit rate, and a liquidity/year breakdown; `backend/app/flow/validate.py`
adds a Wilson interval, AUC and a momentum baseline over at most 30 tickers.

It was **not computed by the first pass**, but it is computed here: 20 liquid
names, 250 sessions, 5-session horizon, bars ending 2026-11-13
(`app.flow.validate`, `horizon=5`, `source="ohlcv"`).

| Measure | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Direction hit rate | **49.40%** (n = 4,140; Wilson 47.86–50.95%) |
| AUC | 0.4912 |
| Baseline, 5-day price momentum | 49.15% |
| Cross-section rank IC, 207 dates | −0.0144 |
| Top-minus-bottom forward spread | −0.074% |

Every line points the wrong way, and none is distinguishable from noise: the
interval straddles 50% and the proxy does no better than plain momentum. The last
scorable call is dated **2026-11-06**, five sessions before the newest bar, so the
measurement describes the first week of November, not today.

That number was not reachable before this pass: `_cross_section` called
`Series.corr(method="spearman")`, which imports scipy, and scipy is not in
`requirements.txt`. Any multi-ticker validation — i.e. the panel the UI shows —
raised `ModuleNotFoundError`. Fixed in `backend/app/flow/validate.py` by ranking
the series first (Spearman is Pearson on ranks), with a regression test; the
figures above come from a clean install of `requirements.txt`.

## 7. The live record: zero trades

`backend/app/paper.py` keeps the only genuine track record — `paper_book`,
`paper_orders`, `paper_fills` — settled session by session from the plan's own
signals. **`paper.book()` returns `None`**: no book was ever opened, so there are
no orders, no fills and no realised win rate. Tonight's plan has 0 candidates, so
this is not a pause in a record; it is the absence of one. Any live accuracy claim
for this system today is unsupported.

## 8. `sound: True` on a five-session-old tape

The plan was computed 2026-11-14 06:52 WIB but priced on 2026-11-06 bars:
`coverage.benchmark 2026-11-06`, `live_appended 0`, breadth 0.1827, verdict
`NO_TRADE`. Five IDX sessions (Mon 9 – Fri 13 Nov) are missing, not six.

`sound` is not a freshness claim. In `backend/app/plan/verdict.py:155–160` the
check is:

> `bars_session is not None and bars_session == exchange_session`
> "Bars end {bars_session}; the exchange's last session is {exchange_session}."

and `exchange_session` is read from **the same benchmark frame** the session came
from — `plan()` passes `next((d.date() for d in reversed(calendar) if d <= session), None)`
with `calendar = frames.sessions()` (benchmark dates). Both sides move together, so
a stale source always compares equal. The check fires only when a ticker's bars run
*past* the benchmark, or when `?as_of=` asks for an earlier session. It never
compares against the wall clock.

The related guard, `bars.session_is_final()` in
`backend/app/plan/bars.py:34–46`, refuses a bar dated *today* before 16:02 WIB — it
protects against a partial candle, not an old one. And `default_source()`
(`bars.py:524`) returns `StoreBars`, else `StudyBars`, else `LiveBars`; `MergedBars`,
the only adapter that appends a live tail, is not in that chain. A missing or empty
store therefore freezes the plan on the study's last session indefinitely, with all
six integrity checks green.

This is the real accuracy problem in the product, and it is one line of code away
from being a data problem rather than a scoring problem.

## Limits

- 20 names, one horizon, one market: the 49.40% flow reading is a proxy on liquid
  tickers, not a census of the universe.
- 76 trades, one market, three trending folds in a 3-year window; the block CI spans
  zero. Everything above is a statement about IDX daily bars, long-only.
- Survivorship runs through the replay and the fold panel: the universe is today's
  listing applied backwards.
- Study prose and study CSVs disagree in places (the corrected headline; the
  capitulation table). Where they do, this note cites the corrected prose and says so.
- `backend/app/plan/method.py`'s `_multiple_testing()` note still says "SPA p = 0.004"
  while it reads **0.0655** from `results/multiple_testing.csv` — stale prose inside
  the app, same failure class as the freshness check.

**Bottom line:** the system does not have a "today" accuracy, because today it made
no call and its data is five sessions old. What it has is a measured rule — 76.32%
win, +1.85% a trade, both of which shrink to +0.04% under the study's own haircut —
and a plan that correctly says do not trade, for a reason (`sound: True`) that
cannot detect the staleness it is sitting on. The one live-scored signal in the
product, the flow proxy, reads **49.40%** against a 50% coin.
